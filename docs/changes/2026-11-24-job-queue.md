# A queue of scans and exports

**Status:** on branch `claude/issue-53-job-queue`, waiting for you to merge.
Closes [#53](https://github.com/Na-bra/flux-cutter/issues/53).

## What changed

The window used to run one job at a time and refuse the next while one ran.
Press **Export**, and until it finished the gallery ignored clicks, the cut
list would not be edited, the pack length would not be typed, and a second
**Export** came back with "already running". Three scene packs of three
people meant sitting through each encode before you could describe the next.

Now they queue. A new strip appears under the progress bar, listing what is
running and what is waiting, in the order it will run:

    RUNNING — 2 WAITING
    ▶  Export scene pack  jamie-pack.mp4              [ Cancel ]
    1  Export reel        sam.mp4            [ ↑ ] [ ↓ ] [ × ]
    2  Scan               episode2.mp4       [ ↑ ] [ ↓ ] [ × ]

- **Add** a job by pressing **Export** or **Scan for people** as usual.
  Neither waits, and neither is refused because something is running.
- **Cancel** the one running, with the button on its row. The job behind it
  starts straight away; a cancelled export leaves no partial file, as it
  always did. While it is stopping the button says **Stopping…** — an export
  finishes the cut it is in the middle of first.
- **×** takes a waiting job out of the queue, so it never runs.
- **↑** and **↓** move a waiting job one place, so the pack you want tonight
  can go ahead of the two you asked for first.

The strip is hidden entirely while nothing is queued, so until you ask for a
second job the window looks exactly as it did.

**The job runs with what it was given, not with what the window shows when
its turn comes.** Pressing Export works out everything the encode needs
there and then — the scan, the people chosen, the cut list as you edited it,
the scene pack length, the encoder, the quality and the file name — and
hands the lot to the job. So while `jamie-pack.mp4` is being cut you can
choose somebody else, drop three of their cuts, type a different pack
length, and press **Export** again: that describes the *next* reel and
leaves the one being cut alone. This is what the old refusals were for, and
why they can go.

**Two jobs still never read the same video at once.** One runner thread
takes one job at a time: not a simplification but the design — two readers
of one video share a file offset and seek each other sideways, and two
encodes are two halves of the same machine, so the second does not finish
sooner for having been started early. The filmstrip and the cut list's
pictures are the one exception, and they already read their own copy of the
file rather than the one an export is holding.

### What the window still refuses while anything is queued

Only the corrections that would change what a queued job is about to do:

- **Merge**, **Split…**, **Name…**, **Not a person** and **Report**, and the
  same four on a cast of several videos;
- **Same person?** answers;
- the **Advanced** settings, and the **People** library;
- taking a video out of the **Videos** set;
- switching between **Video**, **Videos** and **Folder**.

Everything else stays live: choosing people, the cut list, the pack length,
the file name, the format, the encoder, the quality, **Export**, **Choose**
and **Scan for people**.

### Two refusals that are new

- **The same scan twice.** Press **Scan for people** twice on the same video
  with the same settings and the second says "That scan is already in the
  queue." The same video at a different sampling interval is a different
  scan and queues normally.
- **Two exports to one file name.** The second would quietly overwrite the
  first — an encode that ran for half an hour, gone without a word — so it
  says "A job already queued writes sam.mp4. Change the file name first."
  The suggested name follows whoever you chose, so this only catches a name
  you typed by hand and then left alone.

## What it does to your videos and your kept scans

Nothing is written to your videos. Nothing about kept scans, remembered
answers or named people is stored differently, and nothing reads or writes
them at a new time — except one thing, which is a fix:

- **Footage a queued export still needs is no longer closed under it.**
  Scanning another video replaced the scan on screen and closed its file
  handle there and then. With a queue, that handle can be what a queued
  export is about to cut, so the close now waits for the last job that needs
  it. Without this, queueing an export and then scanning something else
  would have cut the reel from a closed file.
- **Kept scans are reused exactly as before.** A queued scan of a video you
  have already scanned still comes back in a fraction of a second.
- **A cancelled export still leaves no file behind**, and a job taken out of
  the queue never touches your disk at all.

## The numbers

**Nothing in `evaluation/baseline.json` can move, so the suite was not
re-run.** No detection, embedding, tracking, grouping, timeline or export
code was touched. The change is in the window's own layer — a new
`app/ui/jobs.py`, plus `app/ui/web.py` and `app/ui/window.html` — and the
evaluation suite does not load any of it: `app/evaluation/run.py` takes one
class (`ScanSettings`) out of `app/ui/worker.py`, which is unchanged, and
`app/ui/web.py` is imported only by the `window` command.

What was measured instead is the queue's own behaviour, as tests, with the
encode faked so no video is decoded:

| | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| A second job asked for while one runs | refused ("already running") | **queued** |
| Three exports queued | not possible | **run in order, three files written** |
| Choosing someone during an export | ignored | **accepted, describes the next reel** |
| Editing the cut list during an export | refused | **accepted, applies to the next export** |
| Typing a pack length during an export | refused ("Wait for this to finish") | **accepted** |
| Cancelling the running job | stops it | **stops it; the next one starts** |
| Cancelling the running job | also cancelled the next one it reached¹ | **leaves the ones behind it untouched** |
| Taking a waiting job out | not possible | **it never runs** |
| Moving a waiting job up | not possible | **it runs sooner** |
| A partial file after a cancelled export | none | **none** |
| The same scan queued twice | — | refused, with a reason |
| Two exports to one file name | second overwrote the first | refused, with a reason |

¹ There was one cancel flag for the whole window, cleared when a job
started. With a queue that would have stopped the job behind the cancelled
one before it had run a frame; each job now carries its own flag.

The whole suite passes: **974 tests, 3 skipped** (931 and 3 before). 51 are
new — 25 in a new `tests/test_jobs.py`, which proves the order, the
removal, the reordering and the cancellation with no bridge and no video at
all, and 26 in `tests/test_ui_web.py`, which drive the queue the way the
window drives it. Eight old tests went, replaced by ones asserting the
opposite, because the window now accepts what it used to refuse.

Three of the new tests load the page's own JavaScript under Node with a
stub DOM and render the queue strip, because nobody can open the window
tonight (below).

## Anything that got worse

1. **The toolbar button no longer turns into Cancel.** It stays **Scan for
   people**, and Cancel moved onto the running job's row — which is where it
   has to be, since "the running job" is now one of several. The row sits
   directly under the progress bar, so it is one line from where the button
   was.
2. **Nothing promises the disk can take it.** Queue six scene packs and six
   files get written; the window does not add up their sizes beforehand.
3. **A queue is not remembered across a restart.** Close the window with two
   jobs waiting and they are gone — the running one is cancelled, as before.
4. **The read that finds where the camera cuts follows the window rather
   than the moment it was queued.** It exists to fill in the window's own
   pack figure, so if you queue it, then scan a different video, it reports
   the new video's cuts. Everything it writes is keyed to the footage it
   actually read, so nothing is mixed up — but it is the one job that is not
   handed its subject up front.
5. **Nothing stops you queueing a lot of work.** There is no limit on the
   queue's length, and no estimate of how long it will all take.

**One thing is not done.** This has not been driven in the real window — the
nightly run cannot open it. Every decision is on the Python side of the
bridge and covered by tests, the page's JavaScript parses, and the queue
strip is rendered under Node and checked row by row. What wants your eye:
that the strip reads well under the progress bar with five or six jobs in
it (it starts scrolling at 30% of the window's height), that the **↑ ↓ ×**
buttons are big enough to hit, that **Cancel** on the running row is
obvious enough to find, and that queueing a second export really does let
you keep working while the first encodes.

## How to undo

Revert the commit. Nothing is stored differently, so there is nothing to
clean up: kept scans, fingerprints, remembered answers and named people are
all written in the same formats and the same places.

To keep the code but go back to one job at a time, make `Bridge._start` in
`app/ui/web.py` refuse while `self._queue.active()` — the window then
behaves as it did, except that Cancel is on the job's row rather than on
the toolbar button.
